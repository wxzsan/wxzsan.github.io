Query Complexity and Computational Hardness

I design quantum algorithms and study lower bounds for simulation, ground-state preparation, and estimation, primarily in the query model. I also study the computational complexity of spectral problems.

Time-Dependent Hamiltonian Simulation with Optimal Query Complexity

Boyang Chen, Minbo Gao, Xinzhao Wang†, and Shuo Zhou.

We obtain optimal query complexity for Lipschitz-continuous time-dependent Hamiltonian simulation, matching the bound for time-independent Hamiltonians. A private-block analysis of catalyst-omission error guides an LCU of transducer-reuse circuits of different lengths, canceling leading error terms and achieving factorial error suppression.

Product-Formula Methods and Error Mitigation

I study commutator-based error bounds for product formulas, and develop compensation and extrapolation methods for reducing Trotter errors and mitigating physical noise in Hamiltonian and Lindbladian simulation.

Lindbladian Simulation with Commutator Bounds

Xinzhao Wang*, Shuo Zhou*, Xiaoyang Wang, Yi-Cong Zheng, Shengyu Zhang, and Tongyang Li.

We establish a general finite-order BCH truncation bound in terms of doubly right-nested commutators, without assuming convergence of the full BCH series. For local Lindbladians, Richardson extrapolation then gives \(\widetilde O(\sqrt N)\) Trotter steps per circuit, with only polylogarithmic precision dependence in the per-circuit gate count for observable estimation.

Other papers

  • Trotter Error Compensation with Polylogarithmic Precision and Nested-Commutator Scaling without Ancillas

    Xinzhao Wang, Shuo Zhou, Ziruo Wang, Pei Zeng, Jinzhao Sun, Qi Zhao, Tom Gur, and Tongyang Li.

    For local Hamiltonians, high-order channel compensation improves the evolution-time and system-size scaling of a \(K\)-th-order product formula to that of order \(2K+1\), with polylogarithmic precision dependence per circuit and no ancillas.

  • Joint Mitigation of Algorithmic and Physical Errors in Noisy Hamiltonian Simulation

    Shuo Zhou*, Xinzhao Wang*, Ruiqi Zhang*, Xiaoyang Wang, Yi-Cong Zheng, Tongyang Li, and Shengyu Zhang.

    Joint Richardson extrapolation cancels leading Trotter-error and physical-noise contributions along a single parameter path.

  • Quantum Singular Value Transformation without Block Encodings

    Shantanav Chakraborty, Soumyabrata Hazra, Tongyang Li, Changpeng Shao, Xinzhao Wang†, and Yuxin Zhang.

    Trotterization and Richardson extrapolation implement QSVT using one ancilla and no multi-qubit controlled gates, with near-optimal commutator-sensitive gate complexity.

Gate Complexity and Quantum Data Access

I study the gate cost of loading classical data into quantum circuits, including sparse QROM, state preparation, and block encoding. I also develop gate- and ancilla-efficient implementations of Hamiltonian simulation and quantum singular value transformation.

Optimal T Counts under Sparsity: from QROM to State Preparation and Block Encoding

Tongyang Li, Fengning Ou, Xinzhao Wang†, Penghui Yao, Pei Yuan, and Shengyu Zhang.

We prove matching \(T\)-count upper and lower bounds for sparse QROM, state preparation, and block encoding. The lower bounds hold even with adaptive measurements and classical control.

Other papers

  • Gate-Efficient Implementation of Query-Optimal Time-Dependent Simulation

    Boyang Chen, Minbo Gao, Zhengfeng Ji, Tongyang Li, Xinzhao Wang†, and Shuo Zhou.

    An exact dyadic factorization yields a gate-efficient implementation of our query-optimal time-dependent Hamiltonian simulation algorithm.

  • Quantum Singular Value Transformation without Block Encodings

    Shantanav Chakraborty, Soumyabrata Hazra, Tongyang Li, Changpeng Shao, Xinzhao Wang†, and Yuxin Zhang.

    Also listed under Product-Formula Methods. Its one-ancilla implementation has near-optimal, commutator-sensitive gate complexity.

  • Randomized Quantum Singular Value Transformation

    Xinzhao Wang*, Yuxin Zhang*, Soumyabrata Hazra, Tongyang Li, Changpeng Shao, and Shantanav Chakraborty.

    Randomized QSVT via importance sampling and qDRIFT replaces explicit block encodings with sampled Hamiltonian evolutions. Its gate complexity is independent of the number of terms and quadratic in the polynomial degree.

Online Learning and Equilibrium Computation

I study multiplicative-weights update (MWU) and related mirror-descent methods in matrix online learning, adaptive learning of quantum states, and quantum algorithms for correlated equilibria in general-sum games.

Instance-Optimal Matrix Multiplicative Weight Update and Its Quantum Applications

Weiyuan Gong, Tongyang Li, Xinzhao Wang†, and Zhiyu Zhang.

We obtain instance-optimal regret for matrix learning from expert advice at the computational cost of standard matrix multiplicative weights. The main technical ingredient is a one-sided Jensen trace inequality for a class of potential functions.
